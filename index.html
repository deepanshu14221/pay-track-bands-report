Same job title. Up to 6x the pay. Here's the switch timeline.

India's software engineer market isn't one market, it's two separate pay tracks running side by side. Your first offer puts you on one of them. This report is the real month-by-month plan to cross from the services track to the product/GCC track.

02Why staying put doesn't close the gap

The median annual increment for staying at the same employer is 8-10%. Three years of annual hikes grows your pay roughly 25-30% total. One well-timed switch from services to product or GCC typically grows it 70-110% in a single move — more than a decade of staying put.

03The 18-24 month switch timeline

MONTHS 1-6
Fix the fundamentals gap
DSA to a consistent 2-pointer/sliding-window/graph comfort level, one real end-to-end project (not a tutorial clone) you can explain in depth, and a resume rewritten around impact, not a task list.
MONTHS 6-12
Build the proof, not just the resume line
A second project with real users or real data, open-source contributions if you can manage them, and your first round of off-campus/lateral applications to GCCs and mid-tier product companies — these have a much lower bar than FAANG and are actively hiring in 2026.
MONTHS 12-18
Target the actual gap companies
GCCs (Walmart Global Tech, JPMorgan, Goldman Sachs, Cisco) consistently match or beat funded startups in cash comp at the 3-5 year mark without equity risk, and are chronically underrepresented in placement-cell conversations. Apply here first, not just FAANG.
MONTHS 18-24
Negotiate like it's a different market, because it is
Product and GCC offers have real negotiation room on joining bonus, stock, and start date. Services offers mostly don't. Know this going into the final conversation.

04The one number that matters more than your CTC

Which track your next offer comes from matters more than what your current one pays. A ₹6 LPA services offer and a ₹14 LPA product offer don't compound the same way — the product track's growth curve outruns the services track's growth curve by design, not luck.
